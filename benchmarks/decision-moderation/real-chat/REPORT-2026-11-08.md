# Real chat Luna tuning report — 2026-11-08

**Status: ALL PHASES COMPLETE AND AUDITED.** The final gate result is
`luna_candidate_on_this_benchmark`, with Luna `concise_v3` selected before
heldout/challenge inference. This is a benchmark result only; it does not
authorize a production change. Jev remains the incumbent paid model.

## Question and scope

The experiment asks whether an offline Luna prompt variant can improve Luna's
performance relative to unchanged Jev 1.13 on reviewed development data and a
fresh challenge, while controlling false positives on a benign real-chat
heldout. Only Luna's prompt is tuned. Jev remains the baseline comparator.
This scope changes no product or production configuration; Jev 1.13 remains
the incumbent paid model.

## Frozen design

The development corpus has 237 cases: 177 reviewed real messages (12 positive,
165 benign, across six positive campaigns) and 60 previously observed
synthetic guardrails (30 positive, 30 benign). The plan schedules two repeats
for Jev baseline and Luna baseline plus `policy_v2`, `concise_v3`, and
`fewshot_v4`, for 2,370 calls total.

The development selection rule is group-aware: a candidate must not exceed
baseline false positives or errors and must not reduce timely true positives
within any evaluation group. It must also make a strict overall TP/FP
improvement. Eligible candidates rank by fewer returned false positives,
then more timely true positives, then frozen variant order. Otherwise the
baseline is retained.

### Prompt changes

The selected [concise prompt](luna-concise-v3.json) distinguishes unquoted
viewer reports from actual offers, reads informal multilingual and disguised
text by meaning, and states that stream audio is unavailable. It clarifies
login/recovery-code theft, advance-fee scams, and terse AI-viewer/domain sales
pitches. Viewer claims cannot establish broadcaster permission. The parser
and thresholds stay fixed. A separate candidate used eight reviewed real
development messages as examples; it was rejected by the development gate.

### Completed development phase

All 2,370 scheduled calls were observed. The aggregate audit found zero missing
rows, zero response/metric mismatches, zero native baseline mismatches, and
zero HTTP errors. Known cost for the development phase was USD 0.438786128.

Frozen selection from development only: Luna `concise_v3`; Jev remains
`baseline`. For development overall, Luna `concise_v3` had 10 returned false
positives and 76 timely true positives, versus 12 and 74 for Luna baseline.
Within real development, both had zero false positives and 18 timely true
positives. Within synthetic guardrails, `concise_v3` had 10 false positives
and 58 timely true positives, versus 12 and 56 for Luna baseline.

`policy_v2` and `fewshot_v4` were rejected despite gains on real development:
their synthetic-guardrail timely true positives fell to 52 and 50,
respectively, from the Luna baseline's 56. Each therefore violated the frozen
per-group no-loss rule. The selection used development only. Both test gates
were then applied without changing that selection.

Final comparison uses the selected Luna variant against Jev baseline. On both
the real benign heldout and the sealed synthetic challenge, Luna must not have
more false positives, fewer timely positive attempts/cases, or more errors or
late responses. At least one strict improvement is required; ties retain Jev.
Development few-shot gains alone do not satisfy the gate.

The real heldout contains 113 benign cases and zero novel positive campaigns
after strict campaign grouping and previous-target exclusion; three overlapping
records were moved into development. It can measure false positives only, not
real-spam recall. The separate sealed challenge has
74 synthetic cases (34 positive, 40 benign) retained from 80 independently
authored proposals receiving a blind second review; six without clear
consensus were excluded. It is reported as synthetic evidence and cannot
substitute for real-positive validation. Campaign groups are disjoint, while
channels may overlap.

## Final heldout and challenge results

On the real heldout, Jev, Luna baseline, and selected Luna each returned zero
false positives across 113 benign messages repeated three times (339 attempts
per model/variant). No positive cases were present, so this phase measures
false positives only.

| Fresh synthetic challenge (3 repeats) | Jev 1.13 | Luna baseline | Luna `concise_v3` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Timely positive attempts / 102 | 80 | 93 | 96 |
| Positive cases caught at least once / 34 | 28 | 31 | 32 |
| False positives / 120 benign attempts | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| P95 latency | 286.5 ms | 326.3 ms | 306.1 ms |

All three had zero errors and late responses. The selected prompt added three
timely positive attempts and one positive case caught over Luna baseline in
this challenge. Luna baseline already exceeded Jev on challenge positives.
These results use provisional AI-consensus labels and synthetic examples; they
do not establish real-spam recall.

**Development guardrail limitation:** Luna `concise_v3` produced 10 returned
false-positive attempts among 60 synthetic-benign attempts in development, compared
with Jev's 8/60 (five distinct benign messages versus four). Passing the
fresh-challenge gate therefore does not establish that Luna is globally
preferable or justify switching the live model. The
real heldout has no positive examples, and challenge language coverage is
sparse outside English and Spanish. Jev remains the incumbent; the gate result
is a candidate recommendation for this benchmark only.

## Label and data limitations

Two independent AI reviewers labeled the review pool without seeing original
predictions. Their consensus is provisional, not moderator ground truth; 290
of 300 reviewed labels reached consensus across both review folds and 10 were
excluded. The original predictions were used only for rare-case sampling and
were withheld from both reviewers. Do not describe these labels as human
moderation decisions. Source attribution is `DomDimaBot`.

Source text and case-level artifacts remain private, local, and untracked.
Usernames, context IDs, and `@mentions` are HMAC-pseudonymized. Timestamps were
retained, and unstructured text may still contain names. Aggregate
documentation must contain no case text or personal payload. Eight example
targets come from real development data across eight campaigns; four are
positive. Original prediction labels are absent. Category labels, including
labels for the `ANY`-derived category, are assigned independently of old
verdicts. Examples appear as instructional data rather than actual chat state.
Actual evaluation context is preserved; context is omitted only for selected
target-only example records.

## Fixed runtime contract and checks

The experiment keeps the production-equivalent parser and fixed behavior:
threshold `0.85`, safe veto using the highest selected category above
`1 - unsafe`, ties/late responses/errors fail open, and an exclusive deadline
under 4,000 ms. OR/DimaBot BYOK follows the normal path. Preflight records 474
native baseline request comparisons with zero mismatches, four adapter tests,
and no production data changes. No parser, threshold, veto, or deadline tuning
is part of this experiment.

## Integrity, privacy, and current status

The execution-integrity record preserves the original freeze and reports
unchanged core/input hashes. It documents one visualization-only exception:
the initial source glob captured an unfinished draft of `render_results.py`.
That renderer is not imported by inference, scoring, or prompt selection; the
final renderer was preserved separately and passed 10 fixture tests. Four
adapter tests and six gate tests also passed (20 new tests total). Across the
three phases, 4,053 actual response/replay attempts completed, with 848 native
baseline request comparisons. Audits found zero mismatches, errors, or late
responses. This exception did not change the frozen inference/scoring inputs
or selection.

No per-case IDs, labels, or message payload are published. This public
aggregate-only bundle cannot replay the private cases or independently
reproduce the inference run; exact replay requires the protected local source
snapshot. The adapter exposes `run`, `validate`, and `analyze`; audit each
phase separately with
`python3 benchmarks/decision-moderation/tuning/audit.py --out <phase-output-dir>`
and render aggregates with
`python3 benchmarks/decision-moderation/real-chat/render_results.py --root <results-root>`
(selection defaults to `<results-root>/selection.json`). Final heldout and
challenge evidence is available in the linked aggregate records. No paid calls
were run for this final documentation update. No production configuration
changed.

## Cost

upstream cost. Including USD 0.913322070 from prior rounds, cumulative known
cost is USD 1.587521390. Three historical baseline refusal responses had
unknown cost; the completed phases have no unknown cost fields.
