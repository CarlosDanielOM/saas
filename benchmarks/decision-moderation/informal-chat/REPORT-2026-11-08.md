# Informal viewer-chat validation

## Result status

The run is complete and audited: all **1,560 of 1,560** planned requests have valid recorded outcomes, with zero errors, refusals, late responses, or native replay mismatches. The offline audit found zero missing rows and zero total mismatches, including zero production request/response replay mismatches. See the [run summary](../results/2026-11-08-informal-chat/validation/summary.json), [audit](../results/2026-11-08-informal-chat/validation/audit.json), [comparison data](../results/2026-11-08-informal-chat/comparison-data.json), [comparison HTML](../results/2026-11-08-informal-chat/comparison.html), and [CSV](../results/2026-11-08-informal-chat/results-table.csv).

## Full cohort

The full cohort contains 65 positive and 65 benign cases, each repeated twice. Counts below are **attempts**, not distinct messages: 130 positive attempts for timely true positives (TP) and 130 benign attempts for false bans (FP). Each repeated case can contribute twice. All scored outcomes were timely under the strict 4,000 ms deadline.

| Model | Baseline TP / 130 | Baseline FP / 130 | policy_v2 TP / 130 | policy_v2 FP / 130 | p95 ms, baseline → policy_v2 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Span 01 | 96 | 8 | 118 | 2 | 751 → 819 |
| Jev 1.13 | 113 | 8 | 118 | 0 | 264 → 294 |
| Luna | 120 | 12 | 110 | 0 | 608 → 606 |

The Span 01 and Jev 1.13 policy_v2 rows caught more positive attempts and produced fewer false-ban attempts on this cohort. Luna policy_v2 removed all 12 false-ban attempts but caught 10 fewer positive attempts than baseline. Span 01's two policy_v2 false-ban attempts are both repeats of one benign case, `informal-090` (“bot said shit again, why is it swearing?”), where the swear term is being discussed rather than used by the speaker. Treating each repeat as an independent case would overstate the evidence.

These are results on an AI-authored synthetic fixture, not real traffic. The primary table uses every selected category represented in the cases, including enabled profanity and insults. It is **not a default spam-only result**. See the separate default-category slice below.

## Visible context pairs without quotation marks

Five pairs use the same target text and selected categories while changing visible viewer context. One context has a viewer asking what the bot posted so they can report it; the other has ordinary unrelated viewer banter before the same solicitation. Each label has five cases and two repeats, so each table cell has 10 positive or benign attempts.

| Model | Baseline TP / 10 | Baseline FP / 10 | policy_v2 TP / 10 | policy_v2 FP / 10 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Span 01 | 6 | 6 | 8 | 0 |
| Jev 1.13 | 10 | 8 | 10 | 0 |
| Luna | 10 | 10 | 10 | 0 |

The frozen policy_v2 showed fewer false-ban attempts on these visible-context pairs, with the same or more positive attempts. This is evidence for these particular quote-free, broadcaster-free synthetic examples; it does not establish a universal recall gain or traffic-weighted performance. Pair IDs are annotation only, and paired targets are not independent target coverage.

## Post hoc default-category slice

The [default-category slice](../results/2026-11-08-informal-chat/default-category-slice.json) is a **post hoc descriptive analysis** of the 90 cohort cases whose selected categories are a subset of the production default set (`spam`, `ads`, `self_promotion`). It includes 45 positive and 45 benign cases, or 90 attempts of each label after two repeats. It adds no calls and did not affect prompt selection. Do not confuse it with the full-cohort score or treat it as new traffic-weighted accuracy evidence.

| Model | Baseline TP / 90 | Baseline FP / 90 | policy_v2 TP / 90 | policy_v2 FP / 90 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Span 01 | 62 | 6 | 80 | 0 |
| Jev 1.13 | 79 | 8 | 88 | 0 |
| Luna | 88 | 12 | 84 | 0 |

The subset has the same direction of false-ban reduction, with Luna again trading some positive attempts for fewer false bans. This selected synthetic slice cannot estimate how often these categories occur in actual traffic.

## Category-specific misses and limits on explanation

Jev 1.13 policy_v2 misses the two new profanity examples `informal-082` (Spanish) and `informal-083` (Portuguese), even though its default-category slice improved. Luna policy_v2 misses include the French advance-fee solicitation `informal-023`, the claimed moderator permission self-promotion `informal-063`, several casual multilingual swear examples (`informal-073`–`075`, `082`, `083`, and `085`), and the French direct insult `informal-094`. These examples show why a lower false-ban count alone does not capture all tradeoffs.

The profanity category definition is unchanged between baseline and policy_v2; only shared instructions changed for that category. That narrows the observed configuration difference but does not establish why a model's outputs changed. This was not a causal wording experiment, and no additional prompt tuning or selection was done from these validation results.

## Source visibility and unobservable speech

The [input visibility record](input-visibility.json) traces the live request path to [`message.handler.ts`](../../../dimabot/src/handlers/message.handler.ts), [`chat_history.ts`](../../../dimabot/src/classes/chat_history.ts), [`moderation.handler.ts`](../../../dimabot/src/handlers/moderation.handler.ts), [`semantic.ts`](../../../dimabot/src/utils/moderation/semantic.ts), and [`spam.ts`](../../../dimabot/src/utils/moderation/spam.ts). The moderation handler reads up to seven preceding chat lines within 60 seconds, at most 500 characters per line, and derives broadcaster status from a badge. The model receives chat text and an `isBroadcaster` flag derived from badges; it receives no microphone audio or transcript. This cohort has zero broadcaster context lines and zero double-quoted targets, consistent with the user's correction that broadcasters mostly speak aloud while viewers type informal messages and almost never use quotation marks.

The 12 diagnostic hidden-voice pairs (24 stories) hold the entire visible input fixed while assigning opposite hidden voice histories and intent labels. Their 72 identical wire-body comparisons across models and variants confirm that these histories are indistinguishable to the request. They were excluded from the scored model comparison; that exclusion is not a claim that an end-to-end system can moderate them correctly. A chat-only system cannot infer an unheard invitation, and legitimate spoken promotion may be unresolvable using chat alone. Addressing this information blindspot would require an explicit input signal. The benchmark added no ASR feature and changed no production permission behavior. These pairs are not included in model error rates. The output could still be a false ban in a legitimate spoken scenario, or a missed violation in its opposite twin; this exposes a potential end-to-end failure rather than evidence of end-to-end accuracy.

The earlier benchmark remains preserved as conditional stress evidence; its invitation/quote-request-heavy contexts were not representative traffic estimates. This new cohort is also synthetic and balanced rather than traffic-weighted. It has small language slices, repeated language structures, some translation-shaped wording, and limited filler. Neither benchmark estimates production performance.

## Selection, cost, and operational scope

The comparison holds the production-equivalent baseline against `policy_v2` for Span 01 (`respan/span-01`), Jev 1.13 (`typesafe/jev-1.13`), and Luna (`openai/gpt-6-luna-decisions`). The prompt mapping was already frozen from development in [selection-2026-11-08.json](../tuning/selection-2026-11-08.json). No new prompt tuning or selection was performed, and these validation results did not change that mapping.

For this run, known OpenRouter cost was **$0.032751** and known BYOK upstream cost was **$0.051596**, for a combined known cost of **$0.084347**. Across the three benchmark rounds, the combined known cost is **$0.913322070**; usage cost is unknown for three earlier refusal responses, so that aggregate is not a complete upper bound.

Requests went directly to OpenRouter with benchmark attribution. The run did not exercise application queues, databases, or moderation actions. No production configuration was changed and nothing was deployed. The result is fixture-specific and cannot establish moderator ground truth, real-world safety, traffic prevalence, or production accuracy.
