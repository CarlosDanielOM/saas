**Chat Moderation** keeps unwanted messages out of your chat. It has two parts:

- **Chat protection** checks messages for spam, scams, and ads. It is on by default for every channel.
- **Rules** are filters you set up for caps, links, emote spam, and blocked words.

It is available on **Free, Premium, and Pro**, and is marked beta in the module catalog. Viewing settings needs the `moderation:view` permission. Changing them needs `moderation:manage`. Without that permission, you can see the settings but not change them.

## Set it up

<Steps>

1. Open **Modules → Chat Moderation**.
2. In **Protection**, choose **What to detect** and **Exempt roles**. Chat protection is already on.
3. In **Rules**, turn on **Rule filter** and add the rules you want. You can add up to 20.
4. For each rule, set **Skip this rule for** and the three steps: **First time**, **Second time**, and **Every time after**.
5. Select **Save Settings**. Changes apply after you save.

</Steps>

The page has four tabs: **Protection**, **Rules**, **Recent Actions**, and **Decisions**.

## Chat protection

Chat protection checks each chatter's first message in your channel for the behaviors you select. A match is a **ban**, applied right away.

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Spam / scams** | On | Scam offers, credential theft, buying fake viewers or followers. |
| **Ads / sales** | On | Offers to sell products, services, viewers, or followers. |
| **Self-promotion** | On | Asking viewers to follow or visit the sender's own channel or social account. |
| **Profanity** | Off | Explicit swear words in the sender's own message. |
| **Insults** | Off | Direct, abusive attacks on a person or group. |

- **Review subsequent messages** (Premium and Pro) checks every message, not only the first one. Each review uses AI credits.
- **Ban confidence** is the score needed before a ban. The default is 85%. Premium and Pro can choose from 80% to 100%. Free always uses 85%.
- Uncertain cases are allowed and logged. They do not count toward the rules' steps.
- **Exempt roles** are skipped. Moderators, editors, and channel admins are exempt by default. You can also exempt VIPs, subscribers, and founders.
- The broadcaster and the bot account are always skipped.
- If you clear every category under **What to detect**, chat protection pauses. **Rule filter** does not turn chat protection off.
- Chat protection runs separately from your rules. The AI review is limited to 30 messages per minute per channel. Messages above that limit are not reviewed.

## Rules

Each rule checks one kind of message.

| Rule | Blocks | Settings and defaults |
|---|---|---|
| **Caps** | Messages with many capital letters | **Caps count** (default 8 capitals) or **Caps percentage** (default 70%). **Minimum message length** counts letters (default 10). Emote names do not count. |
| **Links** | Links to domains not on your list | **Allowed domains** (up to 50). Subdomains are included, so `twitch.tv` also allows `clips.twitch.tv`. A new link rule blocks every link until you add domains. |
| **Emote spam** | Messages with more Twitch emotes than the limit | **Maximum emotes** (default 10) |
| **Blocked words** | Whole words or phrases you list | Up to 200 terms, separated by commas or Enter. Ignores capitals and accents. Does not match inside longer words. |

The link check is a practical filter, not a full URL validator. A link it does not recognize as a URL is not blocked.

New channels start with **Caps**, **Links**, and **Emote spam** on, and **Blocked words** off. Existing channels keep their current rules until they save.

## Penalties and exemptions

Each rule has three steps, and the third repeats for every later offense:

- **First time**, **Second time**, and **Every time after**.
- Each step can be **Just count it**, **Warn**, **Delete**, **Timeout**, or **Ban**.
- The default steps are a warning, then a delete, then a 60-second timeout.

| Action | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Just count it** | Records the offense and lets the message continue normally. |
| **Warn** | Sends a Twitch warning and a public chat notice. |
| **Delete** | Deletes the message and sends a public chat notice. |
| **Timeout** | Times out the chatter for the length you set, up to 14 days. |
| **Ban** | Bans the chatter permanently. |

Public notices appear at most once per chatter per minute. The moderation action still runs for every match.

**Forget warnings after** sets how long an offense counts toward the steps. It is set in minutes, from 1 to 1,440 (24 hours). The default is 60 minutes. Each rule counts offenses separately for each chatter. The timer starts with the first offense, and after it runs out, the next match starts again at **First time**.

**Skip this rule for** sets who is exempt from one rule. Choose one method for each rule:

- **User level**: for example, **Mods and above**, the usual choice.
- **Tags**: exempt or include viewers by role, such as VIPs or subscribers.
- **Accounts**: always exempt, or always apply the rule, for specific Twitch usernames.

The broadcaster and the bot account are always skipped.

## Advanced options (Premium and Pro)

These options are saved on Free, but they do not act there.

- **Word variations** catch spelling tricks for a blocked word. You can match spaced letters, repeated letters, or broader spellings.
- **Regex patterns** extend a blocked word with a pattern. Use RE2 syntax without slashes, lookarounds, or backreferences.
- **Contextual review** asks the AI to check a match against your written policy and nearby chat. Only a confirmed violation counts toward the steps. An uncertain review, a timeout, or no AI credits allows the message and logs it. Reviews use AI credits.

## Permit a temporary bypass

Custom AST commands can call `$(permit)` to bypass the moderation filters, including chat protection, for a short time. With no arguments, everyone in the channel is permitted for 60 seconds. You can permit one user with `$(permit username)`, or set a time with `$(permit username seconds)`. Times range from 1 to 600 seconds. A number alone permits everyone for that many seconds. Restrict any command that uses this function to trusted users. See [AST moderation functions](/commands/advanced/functions/).

## Recent Actions and Decisions

**Recent Actions** lists what the bot did in the last 30 days: the time, user, rule, action, offense number, and result (**Applied** or **Failed**). It shows 10 rows per page.

**Decisions** shows the matches and AI verdicts from the last 30 days. The bot keeps these records for 180 days to improve moderation.

A **Failed** result means Twitch rejected the action, or the bot lacks the moderation permissions it needs in your channel. Check that the bot can moderate your channel, then review the failure before you rely on a rule.

If the moderation system hits an internal error, the bot logs it and the message continues through normal chat. A moderation problem does not stop other chat features.
