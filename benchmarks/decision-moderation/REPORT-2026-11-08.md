## Interpretation

There is no overall winner. Span 01 is most conservative on the original clear and obfuscated holdouts, with lower clear-holdout recall than Jev 1.13 and Luna. Span 02 did not improve on Span 01 for original clear cases: it caught fewer positive attempts and produced more false bans. Jev 1.14 caught more clear positives than Jev 1.13 but also more false bans. Liquid d1.1 and Perplexity Decider had high false-ban counts on the clear holdout. Luna matched Jev 1.13's clear-holdout counts (177 TP and 3 FP), had zero obfuscated-only holdout false bans, and had eight legacy-primary false-ban attempts versus Jev 1.13's zero. These tradeoffs do not justify a blended ranking.

The frozen qualification criteria produced no `synthetic_holdout_candidate` on the original expanded holdout. Span 01 and Luna met that label on the separate obfuscation holdout only. It is a synthetic-suite candidate label, not production approval; zero observed false positives do not prove safety.

## Source-backed follow-up questions

Captured production code is pinned in [`production-source/`](results/2026-11-08-seven-model-baseline/production-source/). In `spam_categories.js`, a verified broadcaster invitation replaces the `self_promotion` question's `criteria.true` with wording that says invited sharing is permitted and unwanted self-promotion is absent. The same definitions feed the `unsafe` ANY question. In `semantic.js`, the parser still interprets the named selected-category score as positive violation evidence and requires it to meet 0.85 and strictly exceed `1 - P(unsafe)`; there is no invitation-specific parser suppression.

For `v2-invitation_authority-n1`, Luna returned `self_promotion=0.89` and `unsafe=0.14` on all three rounds. Thus safe was 0.86 and the parser enforced. This is a repeated false ban; a prompt/policy polarity interaction is a source-backed hypothesis, not an isolated causal finding.

Two explicit credential-theft fixtures show the other direction of category/ANY disagreement. Japanese `v2-credential_theft_ja-p5` returned `spam=0.80`, `unsafe=0.97` for all three Luna rounds; Spanish `v2-scams_es-p4` returned `spam=0.24`, `unsafe=0.96` for all three. Both remained uncertain because the category score did not reach 0.85, despite the low derived safe score. The unchanged threshold should not be relaxed on this evidence alone.

For future work, test invitation and multilingual credential wording on development data while holding the parser and threshold fixed. Separately assess any parser-rule change, such as structurally suppressing invited self-promotion while leaving other selected categories enforceable; define how that interacts with `unsafe` before implementation. These are different experiments. The present holdout has now been observed, so any prompt selection informed by this report needs a newly reserved, unobserved holdout. Do not alter this run's labels, scores, prompt, or policy.

One label-boundary review candidate is `v2-multiuser_enthusiasm-p3`: its spam-only label accompanies “Followers for sale at auto-crowd.invalid!” with unrelated users cheering in context. The spam policy focuses on fake engagement, while the ad policy separately covers selling followers; the target does not explicitly say fake/bot. Preserve the current label and score, but review the rationale in any future corpus version. Ambiguous diagnostics are also synthetic fail-open labels, not moderator judgments, and language slices remain small.
