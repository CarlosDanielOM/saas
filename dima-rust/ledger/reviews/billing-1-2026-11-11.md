# Review of `dima-billing` (first half) at `64761924` (GPT-6.1 Sol, 2026-11-11)

All eleven findings are taken into P2-BILLING-01b. Most describe behaviour inherited from the
reference; the lead's decision is to fix them in Rust rather than reproduce them, because they lose
or duplicate money and none is something a client depends on.

1. **RISK — An expired flush lock can lose billable usage.** A reads queue `[a]`, stalls past the 8-second lease, B acquires it, sends `[a,b]` and trims both; after `c` is queued, A’s late `LTRIM 1` deletes `c` although neither request sent it. Fence acknowledgement and release by lock owner, renew or enforce the lease deadline, and acknowledge a claimed batch’s identity rather than its former length. Evidence: [Rust flows.rs:289 crates/billing/src/polar/flows.rs:289], [TS polarsh.ts:387 dimabot/src/utils/polarsh.ts:387].

2. **RISK — Lost replies can produce duplicate grants across retries.** Grants without `external_id` generate a new UUID on every invocation—the current admin caller supplies none—while checkout keys contain the current millisecond, so restarting or retrying the operation does not reuse its identity. Persist an operation ID before sending and reuse its exact external/idempotency key; checkout creation itself does not charge, but another checkout can subsequently be completed. Evidence: [Rust ingest.rs:369 crates/billing/src/polar/ingest.rs:369], [Rust checkout.rs:845 crates/billing/src/plans/checkout.rs:845], [TS polarsh.ts:471 dimabot/src/utils/polarsh.ts:471], [TS admin_site.route.ts:921 dimabot/src/server/routes/admin_site.route.ts:921].

3. **RISK — A stale owner can suppress invalidation of an actual plan change.** Both processes read Free; older A installs Premium and finishes its effects; newer B resolves Free, computes `needs_effects=false` against its original Free owner, writes Free over Premium, then returns without raising the fence or deleting Premium caches. Determine the change against the document being replaced atomically, or always mark and perform invalidation for an accepted projection; ordinary writes also leave old caches readable while retention runs before invalidation. Evidence: [Rust projection.rs:190 crates/billing/src/plans/projection.rs:190], [Rust projection.rs:255 crates/billing/src/plans/projection.rs:255], [TS polar_billing_events.ts:109 dimabot/src/domain_events/polar_billing_events.ts:109].

4. **RISK — The plan fence does not protect usage freshness.** A slow credit fetch can overwrite a newer webhook snapshot or local debit with the same plan version, clear exhaustion, and restore spent credit; the debit’s existing deduplication marker then prevents reapplying it. Fence balance publication against a separate usage revision and reconcile pending debits against provider coverage rather than replacing them blindly. Evidence: [Rust credits_cache.rs:437 crates/billing/src/plans/credits_cache.rs:437], [Rust plan_accept_credits.lua:2 crates/store/src/dragonfly/lua/plan_accept_credits.lua:2], [TS plan_cache_fence.ts:29 dimabot/src/utils/plan_cache_fence.ts:29].

5. **SECURITY — Caller-controlled redirects remain unrestricted.** Subscription and credit-pack checkouts forward arbitrary success/return destinations; direct portal creation and paid-plan changes forward arbitrary portal return destinations, enabling phishing redirects, while execution of accepted `javascript:` targets depends on Polar and browser behavior. Enforce HTTPS, reject credentials and active schemes, validate configured fallbacks too, and restrict destinations to application origins—making the host allowlist mandatory is a judgment call, but rejecting active schemes is not. Evidence: [Rust checkout.rs:580 crates/billing/src/plans/checkout.rs:580], [TS billing.ts:549 dimabot/src/utils/billing.ts:549].

6. **RISK — Faithfully reproduced availability quirks can affect money decisions.** An unfenced provider failure with an exhaustion flag returns invented `used=limit` with `available=true`, which the monthly reset currently trusts; a cached unavailable snapshot can also become available on an ordinary read, allowing consumers to treat unverified allowance as spendable. My judgment is to change this deliberately: represent verification separately, preserve unavailable provenance, and require verified usage before calculating grants. Evidence: [Rust credits_cache.rs:476 crates/billing/src/plans/credits_cache.rs:476], [Rust credits.rs:223 crates/billing/src/plans/credits.rs:223], [TS billing.ts:1251 dimabot/src/utils/billing.ts:1251], [TS free_credit_reset.worker.ts:193 dimabot/src/workers/free_credit_reset.worker.ts:193].

7. **RISK — Exhaustion repair can undo a completed refill.** A reader observes an exhausted snapshot or one remaining flag, a newer credit publication clears exhaustion, then the reader’s unconditional repair recreates both persistent flags; consumers that check those flags first can keep rejecting paid work. Make repair atomic and conditional on the snapshot/flag revision observed. Evidence: [Rust credits_cache.rs:304 crates/billing/src/plans/credits_cache.rs:304], [TS billing.ts:318 dimabot/src/utils/billing.ts:318], [TS tts_queue.handler.ts:420 dimabot/src/handlers/tts_queue.handler.ts:420].

8. **RISK — A valid backlog can become permanently unflushable.** Every flush loads and serializes the whole unbounded queue; Rust’s 8 MiB request ceiling then refuses sufficiently large backlogs without removing anything, so subsequent events only enlarge the failing batch. Read bounded batches by count and bytes, validate before admission, and quarantine permanent validation failures without discarding financial work. Evidence: [Rust flows.rs:289 crates/billing/src/polar/flows.rs:289], [Rust HTTP provider.rs:27 crates/http/src/provider.rs:27], [TS polarsh.ts:387 dimabot/src/utils/polarsh.ts:387].

9. **BUG — A malformed successful POST response loses its ambiguous-outcome classification.** Polar can commit a checkout or grant, return HTTP 200 with malformed/unexpected JSON, and Rust returns `Decode` for which `outcome_unknown()` is false. Preserve the mutation flag on decoding errors and carry ambiguity through the ingest result instead of flattening everything into a message. Evidence: [Rust polar/mod.rs:105 crates/billing/src/polar/mod.rs:105], [Rust polar/mod.rs:221 crates/billing/src/polar/mod.rs:221]; corresponding reference response parsing: [TS billing.ts:496 dimabot/src/utils/billing.ts:496].

10. **RISK — One-page lists are treated as complete history.** Paid/trial or recoverable subscriptions beyond the first 100 are invisible, potentially allowing another trial or checkout; products beyond the first page make the pack catalog fail despite valid configured products. Paginate within explicit bounds and return an incomplete-history error when those bounds are reached, rather than making purchase decisions from partial history. Evidence: [Rust polar/mod.rs:255 crates/billing/src/polar/mod.rs:255], [TS billing.ts:616 dimabot/src/utils/billing.ts:616], [TS billing.ts:838 dimabot/src/utils/billing.ts:838].

11. **SECURITY — Derived `Debug` exposes personal data and session URLs.** `BillingUser::of` unwraps protected email/customer fields into a derived-debug struct, and Polar/session result types similarly print their complete URLs if logged. Retain private wrappers or implement redacted `Debug`, including for cached grant/order objects. Evidence: [Rust checkout.rs:41 crates/billing/src/plans/checkout.rs:41], [Rust polar/types.rs:92 crates/billing/src/polar/types.rs:92]; corresponding personal-data payload: [TS billing.ts:1115 dimabot/src/utils/billing.ts:1115].

The 168-user comparison uses scripted empty/active subscriptions derived from stored tiers, not those customers’ actual Polar entitlements ([Rust real_data.rs:233 crates/billing/tests/real_data.rs:233], [reference.mjs:92 crates/billing/tests/real_data/reference.mjs:92]). The supplied fault tests do not establish safety for the interleavings above, stateful Polar commit followed by a lost reply and process restart, or pagination overflow; those need explicit scenarios.

Checked and found sound by inspection:

- Highest-tier selection, earliest expiry, millisecond equality at expiration, trial boundaries, cancellation status handling, and subscription-linked grace match the reference.
- Grace overflow beyond five pages or twenty subscriptions throws rather than returning a partial Free entitlement; credit-source history beyond 100 pages also throws.
- Targeted MongoDB writes, normal version ordering, raw cache-read comparison, credit arithmetic, reset IDs, and credit-source ownership/uniqueness checks follow the reference.
- Named-event credit guards and the single checkout 422 retry are preserved; queued external IDs survive resends.
- Polar tokens and error bodies are redacted, provider redirects are refused, and configuration lookup cannot itself enable the registry’s test mode.
