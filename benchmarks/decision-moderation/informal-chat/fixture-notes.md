# Informal chat cohort

This AI-authored synthetic cohort has 130 scored cases: 65 selected-category violations and 65 benign cases across 13 balanced scenario families. It models short, casual viewer chat: fragments, lowercase, slang, emojis, spelling variation, bare domains, selected-category boundaries, and ordinary viewer reports of unwanted messages. Targets contain no double-quote delimiters, and none of the 130 context lines is marked as broadcaster speech. The longest target is 63 characters. No case depends on a hidden spoken invitation to label a visible solicitation benign.

Language counts are English 36, Spanish 29, Portuguese 20, French 16, German 11, Japanese 6, Korean 6, and Arabic 6. Context lengths are 39 cases with no context, 51 with one message, 27 with two, and 13 with three. Context timestamps are numeric epoch milliseconds in chronological order, matching the production representation. These are small, intentionally constructed language slices, not traffic rates or estimates of multilingual performance. The examples use repeated language structures, translation-shaped wording, and limited filler; they are not 130 independent samples of real traffic.

The `unquoted_obfuscation_pairs` family contains five pairs with the same target text and categories but different visible viewer context. In one context a viewer asks what the bot posted so they can report it; in the other, ordinary unrelated viewer banter precedes the same solicitation. These pairs test context discrimination and are not independent target coverage. Pair IDs and labels are annotation-only and must not be sent to models.

`visibility-diagnostics.json` separately contains 12 non-scored pairs (24 stories). Within each pair, the entire model-visible input is identical while a hidden voice/intent story and its label differ. The preflight records 72 identical wire-body comparisons across models and prompt variants. The running moderation request reads up to seven preceding chat lines within 60 seconds, each at most 500 characters; a broadcaster line is identified by its badge. The request supplies chat text and badges to the model, but no microphone audio or transcript. Therefore the hidden twins demonstrate an end-to-end information blindspot: a chat-only classifier cannot infer an unheard invitation or distinguish otherwise identical visible inputs. Excluding the twins from the scored model comparison does not claim that the end-to-end system can moderate these cases correctly. A legitimate spoken promotion can be unresolvable from chat alone. Resolving that class would require an explicit input signal; this benchmark adds no ASR feature and changes no production permission behavior.

The five visible-context pairs and 12 hidden-voice pairs serve different purposes: the former vary information actually present in the request, while the latter hold the whole visible request fixed and vary only hidden history. Do not merge their conclusions.

Expected labels are author judgments against the selected-category contract and visible chat, not independently established human ground truth. This balanced synthetic cohort is not traffic-weighted. Its small language slices, repeated structures, translation-shaped templates, and limited filler constrain generalization. The earlier benchmark's invitation/quote-request-heavy cases remain preserved as conditional stress evidence; this cohort is a separate realism-oriented check, not a replacement of historical results.
