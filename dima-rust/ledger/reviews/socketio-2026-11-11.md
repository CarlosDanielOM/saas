# Review of `dima-socketio` at `1f3f37d5` (GPT-6.1 Sol, 2026-11-11)

Kept because two packages work from it: F-ENGINEIO takes findings 10, 11, 12 and 15 and the engine side
of 14 and 4; F-SOCKETIO-b takes the rest. Decisions by the lead are in `packages.md`.

1. **SECURITY — Socket churn grows an unbounded map.** If a socket leaves while its CONNECT/output is queued, `forget_socket` retains its usage entry; draining later reduces its counters to zero without removing it, so one unauthenticated polling connection can repeat join/disconnect/drain indefinitely. Remove retired entries when their final packet drains: [outbox.rs:175 outbox.rs:175], [outbox.rs:196 outbox.rs:196].

2. **SECURITY — Inbound count limits do not provide a practical memory limit.** A busy socket can retain 256 packets of nearly 1 MB each, with substantially larger parsed JSON allocations, and there is no per-socket byte or global inbound budget; allocation/parsing happens before queue admission. Reserve bounded wire/decoded-memory budgets before parsing and retaining events: [connection.rs:238 connection.rs:238], [codec.rs:202 codec.rs:202], [namespace.rs:466 namespace.rs:466].

3. **SECURITY — Running handlers outlive socket admission limits.** `guarded` cancels only at global shutdown, so disconnecting during a non-returning `on_connect`, event or disconnect handler leaves its task/data alive while new connections obtain fresh slots; concurrent event tasks also continue after `on_disconnect` and can mutate data already recovered by another socket. Give each socket a bounded task owner, explicit deadlines and cancellation/completion rules, and fence old-generation work: [namespace.rs:371 namespace.rs:371], [namespace.rs:500 namespace.rs:500], [namespace.rs:640 namespace.rs:640].

4. **SECURITY — Outbound memory escapes the advertised budgets.** The 16 replaceable slots per socket retain whole packets without charging either byte budget—up to roughly 256 MiB/socket—and Engine.IO’s serialization copies release the tracked `Bytes` while encoded output can still await a slow reader. Charge waiting packets and encoded transport/HTTP output until actual write completion: [outbox.rs:284 outbox.rs:284], [outbox.rs:164 outbox.rs:164], [engineioxide-core/packet.rs:179 engineioxide-core-0.2.2 packet.rs:179].

5. **BUG — Global limits use check-then-increment races.** Concurrent handshakes can all pass `connection_count`, particularly before asynchronously initialized WebSockets are registered; outbound and recovery totals likewise check atomics before incrementing under different local locks. Use reservation permits/CAS accounting before allocation, including pending upgrades: [service.rs:245 service.rs:245], [server.rs:429 server.rs:429], [outbox.rs:217 outbox.rs:217], [recovery.rs:273 recovery.rs:273].

6. **DESIGN — The defaults permit cheap global admission and recovery starvation.** About 512 unauthenticated connections claiming eight pattern names each can exhaust the 4,096-namespace table before checks finish; churn can also evict other channels’ recovery sessions/packets within the same family, while 4,096 transports may be insufficient for legitimate overlays. This is a judgement call: establish production capacity and trusted-address/principal quotas, and isolate recovery eviction between channels: [namespace.rs:332 namespace.rs:332], [recovery.rs:232 recovery.rs:232], [recovery.rs:339 recovery.rs:339], [config.rs:84 config.rs:84].

7. **RISK — Session byte accounting excludes heap allocations.** `size_of::<D>()` counts a `String`, `Vec`, map or `Arc` handle rather than retained contents, so the claimed 16 KiB/session limit does not bound actual memory; there is also no global session-byte counter. Store an explicitly bounded recoverable snapshot with deep accounting, excluding live socket/task references: [recovery.rs:83 recovery.rs:83], [recovery.rs:315 recovery.rs:315].

8. **BUG — Failed storage can produce “successful” recovery with missing events.** Both emit paths ignore `append(false)` and still send the offset-bearing event; an event between 8 and 16 MiB is a simple trigger, and because rejection does not advance the sequence, replay cannot detect that omitted event. Record a discontinuity/invalidate affected recovery, or fail the reliable emission explicitly: [recovery.rs:258 recovery.rs:258], [namespace.rs:284 namespace.rs:284], [namespace.rs:730 namespace.rs:730].

9. **RISK — Recovery is bearer authentication without revalidation.** A stolen PID plus an available/guessable offset restores the session and bypasses `check`, exposing successful recovery as an existence oracle; this matches the reference, including its one-time preview-ticket bypass, but must never accidentally cover revoked-token namespaces. Preserve the explicit Off/OffsetsOnly policies, add a separate pre-replay revalidation hook where required, and replace ordinary secret-string comparison with the charter’s constant-time verification: [namespace.rs:398 namespace.rs:398], [recovery.rs:384 recovery.rs:384], [preview.service.ts:47](/home/dom/Projects/saas/dimabot/src/server/services/tts/voice_preview.service.ts:47), [socket_adapter.ts:8](/home/dom/Projects/saas/dimabot/src/pets/socket_adapter.ts:8).

10. **BUG — The transport patch needs a single-upgrade guard too.** Besides the reported failed-probe session closure and writer `try_lock().unwrap()` panic, simultaneous probes can both pass `is_ws()` because starting an upgrade is an unconditional store; they can contend for the writer lock or close a subsequently upgraded session. I would vendor and pin the crate, serialize upgrade ownership, restore polling on probe failure, and synchronize writer startup/closure: [engineioxide/ws.rs:126 engineioxide-0.17.7 transport/ws.rs:126], [ws.rs:259 engineioxide-0.17.7 transport/ws.rs:259], [socket.rs:302 engineioxide-0.17.7 socket.rs:302]; reference rejects concurrent probes at [server.js:616](/home/dom/Projects/saas/dimabot/node_modules/engine.io/build/server.js:616).

11. **BUG — A six-byte UTF-8 Engine.IO message can panic the reader task.** For example, `é1234` reaches `&value[1..6]` before validating its packet type, slicing inside `é`; unwinding skips normal transport cleanup until another lifecycle mechanism intervenes. Patch `engineioxide-core` to validate the ASCII type first and compare probe bytes safely: [packet.rs:224 engineioxide-core-0.2.2 packet.rs:224].

12. **BUG — An incomplete or oversized polling POST can already execute events.** The streaming decoder dispatches complete prefix packets before receiving/validating the whole body, so a chunked POST can perform work and then stall or exceed 1 MB; the reference buffers the body and does not dispatch an oversized/incomplete request. Buffer the complete request under the wire limit before dispatch: [decoder.rs:81 engineioxide-core-0.2.2 payload/decoder.rs:81], [polling.rs:185 engineioxide-0.17.7 transport/polling.rs:185], [reference polling.js:113](/home/dom/Projects/saas/dimabot/node_modules/engine.io/build/transports/polling.js:113).

13. **BUG — Cancelling an acknowledgement future leaks its slot.** Dropping `emit_with_ack` after insertion bypasses both timeout cleanup and removal, leaving its sender registered until an ACK/disconnect; 32 cancelled calls can permanently disable further requests on that socket. Remove the registration with an RAII guard: [socket.rs:365 socket.rs:365], [socket.rs:394 socket.rs:394].

14. **BUG — Shutdown completion does not establish that every task ended within five seconds.** It permits another second after the deadline, ignores the final wait result, and does not join Engine.IO’s detached writer/close tasks before signalling `done`. Use one deadline and track/await transport completion, retaining `UpgradeGuard::abort` to wake stalled I/O: [server.rs:551 server.rs:551], [engine.rs:97 engineioxide-0.17.7 engine.rs:97], [web/server.rs:368](/home/dom/Projects/saas-rust-v2/dima-rust/crates/web/src/server.rs:368).

15. **BUG — Heartbeat behavior differs despite matching configured durations.** Engineioxide sends its first ping immediately and accepts client-originated Engine.IO PING as a heartbeat response; Engine.IO waits 25 seconds initially and closes revision-4 clients sending PING. Correct timing/direction in the vendor patch: [socket.rs:359 engineioxide-0.17.7 socket.rs:359], [ws.rs:216 engineioxide-0.17.7 transport/ws.rs:216], [reference socket.js:93](/home/dom/Projects/saas/dimabot/node_modules/engine.io/build/socket.js:93), [socket.js:138](/home/dom/Projects/saas/dimabot/node_modules/engine.io/build/socket.js:138).

16. **RISK — Client-file caching needs two safeguards.** Concurrent cold gzip/deflate requests each start compression before the `OnceLock` fills, and missing `Vary: Accept-Encoding` permits a shared cache to reuse the wrong representation—the latter also exists in the reference and is a deployment-dependent judgement call. Make compression single-flight/bounded and send Vary on both 200 and 304 responses: [client_files.rs:110 client_files.rs:110], [client_files.rs:137 client_files.rs:137].
