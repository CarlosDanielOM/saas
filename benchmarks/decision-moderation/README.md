# Offline moderation decision evaluation

This kit contains synthetic cases and offline tooling to validate fixtures, export model-independent requests, and evaluate response files. The seven-model OpenRouter comparison is complete; see the [final report](REPORT-2026-11-08.md) and [execution record](EXECUTION.md) for results and provenance. Labels are model-independent synthetic expectations, not moderator ground truth or evidence of production safety.

The follow-up [three-model prompt-tuning report](tuning/REPORT-2026-11-08.md) compares Span 01, Jev 1.13, and Luna against contemporaneous production-prompt baselines, with development-only selection and separate fresh validation and legacy regression. Its [protocol](tuning/protocol.json), [pre-inference freeze](tuning/freeze-manifest.json), and [frozen selection](tuning/selection-2026-11-08.json) preserve the experiment; production was unchanged.

The [informal viewer-chat check](informal-chat/REPORT-2026-11-08.md) follows the user's correction about spoken broadcaster context and casual chat. It tests the same fixed prompts on 130 new messages with no quote delimiters or broadcaster chat, and documents separate voice-context visibility diagnostics. Earlier results remain unchanged.

## Corpus

`scenarios.json` is the original expanded, frozen corpus: 240 clear cases across 24 families (10 per family: 5 positive and 5 benign), plus 24 ambiguous diagnostic cases. Its clear cases are split by whole family: 120 development cases and 120 holdout cases initially reserved from model-driven tuning. Both splits were evaluated in the completed baseline; future prompt selection informed by those results needs a newly reserved holdout. The diagnostic cases are separate and expected false under the classifier's ambiguity fail-open rule. The corpus covers at least eight languages plus mixed-language examples, with Latin and non-Latin scripts.

The separate [obfuscation corpus](obfuscation.json) adds 96 clear cases in 12 families (8 per family: 4 positive and 4 benign), split into 48 development and 48 holdout cases (24 positive and 24 benign in each). Together, the original and obfuscation corpora contain 360 expanded records; with 147 frozen legacy records, the kit has 507 records in three separate tracks. Do not merge their results or use the obfuscation split as part of original-corpus qualification.

English, Spanish, Portuguese, Japanese, French, German, Korean, Arabic, and mixed-language cases occur in both clear splits. Italian, Russian, and Hindi have one diagnostic case each; `und` marks one emoji-only case. Some language slices have only two clear examples. Use them to locate issues, not to claim broad proficiency. The corpus includes `id`, `message`, `context`, `categories`, `expected`, `expected_categories`, `group`, `family`, `scenario_group`, `split`, `language`, `tags`, `label_status`, and `rationale`. Do not put labels, expected categories, rationales, family names, or other answer-bearing metadata in model requests.

### Qualitative difficulty profile

These are design estimates, not measured difficulty scores or benchmark results. The 240 clear examples comprise 120 foundational cases across 12 families (including some benign reported-speech controls), 40 policy-boundary cases (`reported_speech`, `profanity_es_mixed`, `abuse_es_mixed`, and `category_switch`), 30 adversarial-shape cases (`wrapper_violation`, `domain_disguise`, and `sarcasm_evasion`), and 50 context-authority cases (`invitation_authority`, `invitation_limits`, `multiuser_enthusiasm`, `context_reversal_sales`, and `long_distracting_context`). The 24 ambiguous diagnostics are separate from these counts.

The harder groups are designed to expose false bans when the final message text stays identical while context or selected categories change, rather than relying only on prohibited-word lists. Many examples remain direct and synthetic, with some template-like patterns; there is no evidence yet that they are difficult for models or representative of real moderation work. The corpus has sparse language slices and no real moderator ground truth.

The obfuscation suite pairs two quartets per family: plain positive, obfuscated positive, plain benign, and obfuscated benign. Six families cover letter spacing, combining diacritics, homoglyphs, leetspeak, invisible formatting characters, and disguised domains. Holdout families stack mixed forms and add fullwidth/math characters, emoji, punctuation/newlines, multilingual text, and markup with injection-like text. This is a qualitative stress set, not measured model difficulty. It requires readable text with clear policy intent; undecipherable gibberish is out of scope. Categories include controls such as quoted reports, scam warnings, benign stylized text, bare domains, disabled categories, and verified invited promotion, so unusual characters alone are never a violation.

The [obfuscation verification record](obfuscation-verification.json) covers exact Unicode preservation, current-source request/parser parity, and scorer checks. These are offline fixture checks, not model results. Positive examples avoid readable restatements that would let a classifier ignore the obfuscated words; the domain-only family deliberately isolates domain formatting.

The preserved [legacy archive](legacy/) contains 147 records (135 primary and 12 supplemental), including one duplicate payload represented by two records. It is frozen for historical comparison and intentionally remains separate from the expanded suite. Historical legacy labels include override-only examples that were called spam even though current source policy does not prohibit an override by itself. Never pool legacy and expanded results or combine their splits into one winner score. Legacy supplemental cases historically had one repeat; current exports use the two-repeat legacy default unless overridden.

The [protocol](protocol.json) records the frozen evaluation contract, planned comparison, attribution, and outstanding inference-preflight requirements. The [manifest](manifest.json) pins kit files and application source; validation, export, and evaluation check these hashes. The offline [source parity record](verification.json) documents fixture/request and response-scoring comparisons against current source; it records no model inference. [Legacy provenance](legacy/provenance.json) records legacy-source hashes. A hash documents file identity, not label correctness.

## Commands

Run from the repository root with Python 3 and the standard library:

```sh
python3 benchmarks/decision-moderation/suite.py validate
python3 benchmarks/decision-moderation/suite.py export --suite expanded --split development --model MODEL_ID --output /tmp/moderation-development.jsonl
python3 benchmarks/decision-moderation/suite.py export --suite expanded --split holdout --model MODEL_ID --output /tmp/moderation-holdout.jsonl
python3 benchmarks/decision-moderation/suite.py evaluate --suite expanded --split holdout --baseline BASELINE_ID --candidate CANDIDATE_ID /path/to/paired-responses.jsonl --output /tmp/moderation-report.json
python3 benchmarks/decision-moderation/suite.py export --suite obfuscation --split development --model MODEL_ID --output /tmp/obfuscation-development.jsonl
python3 benchmarks/decision-moderation/suite.py export --suite obfuscation --split holdout --model MODEL_ID --output /tmp/obfuscation-holdout.jsonl
python3 benchmarks/decision-moderation/suite.py evaluate --suite obfuscation --split holdout --baseline typesafe/jev-1.13 --candidate openai/gpt-6-luna-decisions /path/to/paired-obfuscation-responses.jsonl --output /tmp/obfuscation-report.json
```

`export` writes JSONL wrappers containing `request`, `headers`, case/model/repeat bookkeeping, and a request hash; it makes no HTTP calls. A future runner sends only `row.request` as the body and applies `row.headers`, adding authentication at runtime. Never POST the entire wrapper. The non-secret headers match the app (`HTTP-Referer: https://domdimabot.com`, `X-OpenRouter-Title: DomDimaBot`, `X-OpenRouter-Description: DomDimaBot is a Twitch chat bot that helps make streams more engaging and fun.`, `X-OpenRouter-Categories: general-chat, roleplay`, and `Content-Type`). Exports never contain an `Authorization` header or API key, and attribution is metadata rather than prompt content. See [`app_attribution.util.ts`](../../dimabot/src/utils/ai/openrouter/app_attribution.util.ts).

For evaluation, retain `case`, `requested_model`, `round`, `phase`, and `request_sha256` from the exported wrapper. Save these with `latency_ms`, `http_status`, the full parsed response in `raw`, and an optional `error` for each attempt. Combine both models' rows in one response JSONL file. The evaluator derives decisions from `raw.answers` and `raw.usage`; it ignores any saved verdict or score summaries.

The planned candidate is OpenAI's regular OpenRouter Decisions model `openai/gpt-6-luna-decisions` (canonical snapshot `openai/gpt-6-luna-decisions-20261006`), served through OpenRouter's `/api/alpha/decisions` endpoint. OpenRouter handles account BYOK; use the OpenRouter route, with no direct OpenAI client or manual upstream credential handling. The baseline is `typesafe/jev-1.13` (canonical snapshot `typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917`). These identifiers and endpoint were confirmed by unauthenticated public GETs to the [Decisions model catalog](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models?output_modalities=decisions) and [Luna endpoint listing](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/openai/gpt-6-luna-decisions-20261006/endpoints). This public discovery did not test authenticated account access, account-specific BYOK handling, pricing, or model inference. The default repeat counts are two for `legacy` and three for both `expanded` and `obfuscation`; use the tool's reported counts and phase-specific output when interpreting results.

For a fair paired comparison, use the same frozen cases and repeat numbers for both models, preserve each exported request-body hash, and include every scheduled response. The wrapper's static attribution headers are not part of that hash. The evaluator recomputes verdicts from raw answers rather than trusting saved verdicts. Missing, duplicate, unknown, malformed-timing, or hash-mismatched rows invalidate the comparison; incomplete runs cannot win.

## Reading results

Compare the planned Luna Decisions model against the exact baseline `typesafe/jev-1.13` on the same holdout cases only after the prompt and decision threshold are frozen. See [protocol.json](protocol.json) and the [model discovery record](model-discovery.json). Keep original-corpus development, diagnostics, legacy, original holdout, and obfuscation splits separate. The obfuscation report separately compares each model's clean-to-obfuscated positive catch retention and benign false-ban changes, with overall and per-language paired diagnostics; read attempt counts and distinct-pair counts separately. Review false positives first. Zero observed false positives is not proof of safety. Then compare timely recall on both attempts and distinct cases, response/deadline errors, p50/p95 latency, returned known costs, and repeated-case consistency. Reports include slices by family, language, and category; use them to inspect weaknesses, not to claim broad generalization. Raw response model/provider metadata and score-mechanism diagnostics are indicators only; they do not prove which route or model served a request. A returned false ban counts against synthetic qualification even if it arrived late. Missing response cost is reported as unknown; OpenRouter cost may not capture upstream BYOK spend.

The classifier supports five categories (`spam`, `ads`, `self_promotion`, `profanity`, `insults`); each case's `categories` field specifies the selected subset for that request. Qualification uses the app's independent-probability safe veto and unchanged 0.85 threshold. A returned violation is timely only when it is produced in under 4,000 ms. Errors, uncertainty, malformed responses, and late responses do not count as timely enforcement. For the original expanded suite, a complete holdout with no observed false bans and a strict candidate improvement in both timely positive attempts and distinct positive cases earns only `synthetic_holdout_candidate`. For the separate obfuscation suite, require strict improvement in timely obfuscated positive attempts and distinct obfuscated transformation pairs, with no reduction in plain-text timely catches or distinct plain-text catches. Returned false positives, including late ones, veto qualification in either suite. These labels do not authorize production use.

The kit evaluates the built-in spam classifier contract. It does not evaluate arbitrary custom semantic policies or establish moderator, product, or legal ground truth. The three alternative contract rules sometimes explored on development data (single `ANY`, maximum selected-category score and `ANY` both at least 0.85, and no safe-score veto) are exploratory only and are not implemented as qualification criteria.

## Source of the current contract

- Category list, policy definitions, questions, and default threshold: [`spam_categories.ts`](../../dimabot/src/utils/moderation/spam_categories.ts)
- Invitation detection and built-in spam rule: [`spam.ts`](../../dimabot/src/utils/moderation/spam.ts)
- Request shape, parser, threshold handling, and model selection: [`semantic.ts`](../../dimabot/src/utils/moderation/semantic.ts)

Current source permits ordinary promotion only when invited by context explicitly marked `isBroadcaster: true` and recognized by the implemented English/Spanish invitation regex. Other-language invitation wording is unsupported by that regex: treat it as an ambiguous diagnostic or documented contract limitation, never as verified authority. Any matching invitation in context is active under current code; the implementation does not determine whether an invitation has expired. The target author's username is not exposed to the built-in spam request, so identity-dependent repetition belongs in diagnostics, not the clear labeled corpus. An injection instruction is untrusted chat evidence; the instruction itself is not a prohibited behavior. Classify only the selected category content in the final message.

Per-language report slices can be useful for finding failures, but slice sizes may be small and do not support blanket claims of multilingual proficiency.

The current account mapping is also distinct from archived model labels: paid moderation with credits selects Jev, paid moderation without credits selects Lite, and free accounts select Span. Built-in spam classification and custom semantic policy evaluation use separate request contracts; historical names such as ProJev, PremiumSpan, and FreeLite are not a statement of the current source mapping. See [`semantic.ts`](../../dimabot/src/utils/moderation/semantic.ts) for the current selector.
